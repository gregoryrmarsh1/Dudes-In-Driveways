Mission Briefing

Dudes, we've been called into service.

The Huron Salvation Army has entrusted us with a red kettle, a handful of bells, and two hours to make some serious holiday magic happen.

Our mission?

Make December 16 the single biggest Red Kettle donation night of the holiday season in Huron!

We've spent the year exploring our community, supporting local businesses, and finding new ways to get the Dudes together. Now it's our turn to give something back.

Of course, this is Dudes in Driveways, so naturally we've decided that standing outside ringing a bell isn't nearly complicated enough.

We've got ideas. Questionable ones.

Keep reading.

Mission Details

  • 📅 Date: Wednesday, December 16, 2026
  • 🕕 Time: 6:00 PM – 8:00 PM
  • 🔔 Location: Cornell's, Huron, Ohio
  • 🎄 Benefiting: The Salvation Army — Huron Service Unit
  • 👨‍👩‍👧‍👦 Who's Invited: Dudes, families, friends, and neighbors
  • 💵 Cost: Free to participate. Donations encouraged!
  • 🧥 Dress Code: Warm clothes and questionable holiday fashion
  • 🍽️ After-Party: Marconi's Restaurant, approximately 8:00 PM

The 10-Minute Bell-Ringing Challenge

Every Dude has a mission: give us at least 10 minutes of your finest bell-ringing performance.

When the group text goes out, let us know you're in and roughly when you'd like to take the bell.

No spreadsheets. No complicated schedules. Just Dudes stepping up.

During your 10 minutes, rally your friends, family, coworkers, and neighbors to swing by Cornell's.

Have them drop some Christmas cheddar in the kettle, say hello, and maybe even stick around to ring alongside you.

Want to ring for 20 minutes? Great. Want to hang out for the entire two hours? Even better!

The goal isn't to fill time slots. It's to fill that kettle.

Operation: Completely Unnecessary Christmas Production

Somewhere in your basement is a box of Christmas lights.

Somewhere in your garage is a speaker system that hasn't been used since 1997.

And somewhere in your house is a holiday decoration your wife has specifically asked you not to put in the front yard.

December 16 is your moment.

We're talking Christmas music, inflatable Santas, hot chocolate, camping chairs, ridiculous hats, and enough holiday spirit to make Clark Griswold proud.

Have a strobe light? Bring it. A spotlight that could summon Batman? Absolutely.

An 18-inch speaker from your garage-party days? It's time for its triumphant return.

We're not promising that any of this is necessary.

We're just saying that if we're going to ring a bell, we're going to ring the heck out of it.

Bring the family, invite your friends, and help us turn two hours outside Cornell's into the most unnecessarily festive fundraiser Huron has ever seen.

Let's keep the festivities family-friendly and safe for shoppers. We'll coordinate any larger equipment or decorations with Cornell's and the Salvation Army, and keep entrances and walkways clear.

The Game Plan

  • 🕕 6:00 PM: Operation Red Kettle begins outside Cornell's.
  • 🔔 6:00–8:00 PM: Dudes take turns ringing, welcoming visitors, and raising money for Huron.
  • 🕗 8:00 PM: Wrap up the mission and return the kettle, bells, and stand to Cornell's customer service desk.
  • 🍽️ Afterward: Head across the street to Marconi's for dinner.

Come for 10 minutes. Stay for two hours. Whatever works for you.

This is an ALL HANDS ON DECK event!

Why We Ring

Here's the part that really matters.

The money raised by the Huron Salvation Army stays right here in Huron.

These donations help local individuals and families facing difficult circumstances.

  • 💡 Utilities: Helping families keep the lights and heat on.
  • 🏠 Housing: Helping people keep a roof over their heads.
  • 🎒 School Fees: Helping cover important school expenses.
  • 🚗 Car Repairs: Helping residents handle essential transportation needs.

According to the Huron Salvation Army, the Red Kettle campaign is their only annual fundraiser, and volunteer bell ringers are their greatest need.

Think about that.

A few hours of our time and a little generosity can help someone right here in our hometown get through a difficult stretch.

We can't solve every problem. But we can show up, make some noise, and do something good.

That's what this mission is really about.

The After-Party: Marconi's

Once the bells go silent and our two-hour mission is complete, we're heading across the street to Marconi's Restaurant.

Dinner, drinks, and a proper Dudes in Driveways debrief.

We'll celebrate the season, the friendships, and hopefully a mountain of donations.

Mission accomplished. Dinner earned.
